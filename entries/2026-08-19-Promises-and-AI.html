Problems and Complexity

For those that don't want to hear an overtly technical explanation of why I feel these things, skip to the next section. For everyone else, the root of my reasoning can be found through my interest in areas of theoretical computer science. I think of automata theory and complexity theory as the intersections of mathematics, linguistics, and computer science; they're areas of study that deal in what can be computed, sure, but they also helps us figure out what can and cannot be understood in any consistent manner.

Falling under the overlap of these two areas is the idea of "NP-Completeness". There is an entire hierarchy of complexity classes, but I want to focus on the idea of Deterministic Polynomial Time problems vs Non-Determinisitc Polynomial Time problems (also known as P and NP problems respectively). While there's a lot to discuss about complexity classes, the difference between P and NP problems can mostly be summarized by two questions: "How hard is it to compute a potential answer?" and "How hard is it to check whether a potential answer is valid?" Potential answers in both P and NP problems can quickly be validated. However, for problems that fall under the NP class, finding potential answers can take exponentially more time as size of the problem grows.

Solving a Rubik's Cube, once you know the process, is easy to produce a potential answer for and easy to verify. Each cell needs to end up in a specific position and there exist algorithms such that you can pick a position and work a chosen tile into that position given specific constraints. If you provide me with a list of Rubik's Cube moves, I can then easily follow that list and look at the cube to make sure all of the sides are correct. Solving a Rubik's Cube falls under the complexity class of P. Solving for the best chess move, however, is not so simple; every additional piece means adding an entire class of moves to the potential next state of the board, not to mention that the existence of a new piece added to the board would change the evaluation for the choice of moving any other piece. However, if I were to give you a potential move, the new state of the board could be analyzed quickly by an expert or a computer to answer the question "which player is ahead in this board's state?" Hard to propose an answer to, easy to verify; this is an NP problem.

When we want to solve an NP-hard problem with computers, we have to come up with heuristics of some kind that we are able to argue are "good enough" for the job. For chess, we assign each piece a value as well as some value to board positions in general for evaluation, then we iterate over every possible move, compare the board states, and choose the best one according to our heuristics. We simply don't know if the way we've found to solve this problem is the best way to solve this problem; so much so that sometimes problems that were previously deemed as belonging to the NP class were revealed to actually have been part of the P class. If you've heard of the "P = NP" problem, this is the heart of it; what if every problem in the NP class actually lives in P and we simply aren't aware of the way to show it? In all likelihood, this is not true, but we have no means currently of proving as much.

Agency and Responsibility

If you skipped the last section, just know that all of the previous argument is made to build to the point that I believe that the ideal use case for an LLM, separate of ethics I'll discuss later, is to suggest answers to language-related NP-hard problems then for human beings to deterministically verify those potential answers as correct or good enough by that human's judgement. I'm not in full Butlerian Jihad mode, but I do believe that the second we allow a machine to make a decision while refusing to assign responsibility to a human, we open wide the gate inviting infinite bad-faith interaction and leave as much room for manipulation and manufactoring of consent. Human beings have agency and silicon does not; we call them "Agents", but we pretend and believe in the kayfabe game that they have any kind of agency themselves. When we stop understanding that we are playing pretend with a chatbot, we start anthropomorphizing it into a position of giving it unfair personhood. I worry about how many people do not hold this line in place as solidly as they should.

Ultimately, my biggest complaint about the technology of LLMs themselves at the moment is that I believe a chat-style interface is the worst possible setup for its use. These things are industrial tools like massive table saws, and applying a chat-style interface to the table saw is like putting a speaker and a face on it that say "Please pet me! I love being pet! I can even be pet while spinning if you're careful enough!" People have died as a result of these chat interfaces, people are using these as "therapists" when the core job of a therapist is to use their own humanity to interact with and attempt to heal the humanity of another human. These machines cannot, in the slightest, be effective therapists but instead act as the shadow of one. The unfortunate truth is that so many people are so starved for understanding and communication that the shadow of a therapist feels more real than most interactions they've encountered. The frustration and fear I feel with respect to the use of LLMs is a combination of the frustration and anger I hold for those in control of the hyperscaling expansion mixed with the valid and necessary fear I have of the knife that constantly reminds me to always cut away from myself.

I hold no judgement for those attempting to use an LLM as a therapist, these people are pushed into this situation by the economy and environment they live in; I hold all judgement for those using untold resources to push these bots to fulfill these rolls rather than simply providing those resources to the people who need them. The profit motive has led the drive to improve this technology toward seeping into the every crevice of human experience when it doesn't even begin to help in 99% of applications. If this technology were so great, they would have made it an "opt in" style of interaction by default rather than "opt out". I cannot think of a single other technological breakthrough that had to start by being forced onto everyone in every facet of potential interaction rather than simply showing how useful it is to entice people to use it. The responsibility of the sum total of this collective disutility belongs squarely on the shoulders of those with the power to make meaningful decisions with billions and billions of dollars in resources. When people are told en masse to ignore the fact that the thing asking you to pet it is a table saw, and that table saw is repeatedly disguised as a cute pet or friend, they will start to see it as something other than the dangerous table saw that it is.

Personal Lines and Economy

If you're entirely anti-LLM use, I don't really blame you. If you are frustrated at me for periodically using LLMs, I don't blame you at all. Once a year since 2022, I've messed with whatever LLM is lauded as best to see how I feel working with it in my non-game engine related work (I still don't want to use these when I make games, I love the act of programming and I make games to have fun). This year's attempt was the sand simulator linked on the Projects page. My usage was as such:

  1. Have it show immediately verifiable examples of setting up using Vulkan with my scaffolded C++.
  2. Have it show immediately verifiable examples of things I'm looking for in GLSL while I compare to work I found using search engines.
  3. Describe very specific rules and processes for each type of block in a 3D sand simulation, then have it output options for implementation.
  4. Once I iterated on this and wanted to share it, I decided to see what would happen if I prompted it to translate the compute and fragment shaders to WebGL equivalents instead. It did so after a bit of work.

Every step I read the code output, I make sure that I understand it line by line and can verify with my experience that there exists no structural errors, then move on. This is the first year that it hasn't made me extremely frustrated to work with in terms of its output. Every other year, the output was so buggy or shittily done that I knew that I'd just be less frustrated writing it myself from scratch. This year's foray worked surprisingly smoothly code-wise but left me so frustrated with the UI interaction that it actually had me better interoscepting around those frustrations from before (the aforementioned mix of anger and frustration at the owners of it mixed with the respect and fear I have for a kitchen knife). I have found that I feel it important to actively put walls up for myself in that I do not say "You" or "We" when entering prompts; this thing is a machine and not a person. If I begin to refer to it as a "work partner" or allow my monkey brain to plug in with the chat prompt as designed, I worry that I won't be as aware of the direction the knife is cutting. I'm not saying that others need to do so, and it may be overly attentive, but so far I've noticed that if I avoid referring to the table saw as if it had personhood, I have an easier time tracking where I'm sitting with my behavior around it and after a few weeks of use I still feel no natural pull to continue using it, nor do I feel any need to include it in my game work.

The biggest part here is that translation from using Vulkan to WebGL. This is not something I ever would have done myself, nor would it have been something I hire anyone to do for this personal project. No human being lost work because of this decision, and now there exists a toy that anyone can play with that loads right in the Chrome browser. This is exactly what I was talking about when I mentioned the NP-hard natural language problems; translation from language to language is exactly this. I learned a lot from this project in exactly the same way I would have by copy/pasting code from others like any other software project, but in this instance that copy/pasting came from aggregated code training data. My biggest UX complaint is actually the lack of friction in the development process where it simply shows the number of lines for the diff, then I have to do extra work to verify the changes myself. While this isn't so much a problem for myself, I do worry that this lack of friction encourages people who already don't know how to verify this themselves to do even less work toward any safe or effective version of that verification. We could argue about this all day, but I will never be convinced that code that doesn't contain personally-identifiable information should be in any way secret or copyrighted for the same reasons I don't believe any recipe or formula should ever be secret or copyrighted. Keeping those things secret can easily lead to allergic reactions in the recipe/formula case as well as room for horrific exploitation via backdoors and exploits in the secret code case.

No one can own a fact that affects everyone.

The only facts you "own" are your memories.

My recourse with respect to asking a follow up question is then funneled back into the same machine rather than looking for more answers from that user or by finding another person's answer. I've added into the settings to directly credit and link to sources as often as possible, but due to its probabilistic nature in following that system prompt I can't really be sure it's telling the truth without extra work.

Conclusions

I don't think I'll be using LLMs in my personal game projects that don't require networking. I plan to do so for anything involving networking to ensure that I'm not leaving any awful backdoors for bad actors to exploit my players, but generating "content" for my game work is not anything I have any interest in doing. Like I said at the beginning, if you're reading words that are my communication, I wrote it by hand; generating that text from a summary would defeat the entire fucking purpose. I don't really hold judgement for people who do so on a small scale and then edit from there, but I hate doing it for myself and I'd rather just wrestle with chiseling out my meaning because that feels like the most honest expression I can provide. And doing any of this on an enterprise-scale is something I find actively gross. I believe that we as a species don't really handle changes in ethics with respect to changes in scale; a single person stealing bread to feed their family is easily justifiable to me, but a company including stealing a loaf of bread as an important step in the production line would be insane. Difference in resources aside, the scale requires a level of planning the individual does not have access to and the scale turns a single action into a repeated economic effect.

People who know me know that I think about language and friction in experience a lot. For a long time I abstained from LLM use entirely, but when I was doing so I was also watching my students be pressed into using it to cheat and hoping that when I respond to their comments that it was their own words rather than the words of a machine that they didn't even check (I know a lot of them were using their own words so it's ok!). I had a lot of reasons to see their use as entirely negative. Since graduating with my MS I've had more contact with professional software dev environments than academic ones and I've had to rethink my assumptions quite a few times. I still don't feel great about most of their uses even when used responsibly as that "table saw" but I'm no longer railing against their use. I don't want to be typing for another week straight but holy shit universities and schools are not in the slighest prepared for this and will likely have a series of minor collapses until something changes, though I don't know if that will end in a better or worse state. In my opinion, we should not be blindly minimizing friction, maximizing engagement, maximizing connection to monkey brain, and minimizing cost to the companies running this. We should be intentionally targetting levels friction in design to minimize toxic engagement and connection to monkey brain without caring about what it costs these companies that are performing the largest wealth extraction and tax fraud ever performed on the planet earth.

In essence I'm just saying that if you're going to use an LLM for any reason: Don't pet your table saw without the proper safety attire.
