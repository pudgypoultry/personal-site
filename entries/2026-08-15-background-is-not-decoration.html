Backgrounds are supposed to be scenery: a texture your eye stops noticing after the first paint. The one behind this page is a game of Life, ticking at a step and a half apart. Every cell you see was placed by your cursor, or by a handful of small colonies I scatter when the page loads.

The tick rate is long on purpose. A Life simulation running at animation rates competes with the reading you are here to do; at this cadence it hovers at the edge of what your peripheral vision catches. You look up, notice the pattern has shifted, look back down. The board changes at the speed of noticing, not the speed of watching.

The other thing I care about is what the board starts as. My first attempt scattered random cells. Almost all of them died on the next tick, because a lone cell has zero neighbours and Life needs at least two to survive. Now the board starts on shapes that persist: blocks, blinkers, gliders, and sometimes a Gosper gun that keeps producing more gliders as long as it survives.

Every seeded shape lasts. Nothing is wasted on the void.

The cursor is the only other input. Move it across a cell and that cell switches on; the next tick decides what happens next. Sweeping across empty space is how you feed the board. Sweeping across a busy region is how you disrupt it. Either way, the reason you touched the page becomes part of what it is showing.
